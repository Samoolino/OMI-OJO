# S9.2 — Governed Scale Control Effectiveness and Exception Automation

## Objective
Automate detection and routing of control/evidence exceptions while retaining authorized human decisions for material severity, CAPA closure, scope suspension/resumption and claims.

## Exception lifecycle
**DETECTED → CLASSIFIED → ASSIGNED → CONTAINED → INVESTIGATING → CAPA_REQUIRED → REVIEW_PENDING → CLOSED**

Exceptions may become **REOPENED** or **SUSPENDED**.

## Automated detection
Automation may detect:
- schema/version mismatches;
- provenance gaps;
- timestamp/order inconsistencies;
- site identity mismatches;
- custody anomalies;
- DMRV reconciliation anomalies;
- anchor digest mismatches;
- stale source/API signals;
- unsupported claim scope;
- overdue control reviews;
- repeated exception patterns.

Automation creates evidence and routes work. It does not convert an anomaly result into a regulatory or environmental conclusion.

## Human authorization
Human authority remains required for:
- material severity confirmation;
- containment approval;
- CAPA acceptance;
- closure authorization;
- scope suspension/resumption;
- public/investor claim approval.

## Fail-closed
An exception cannot disappear because a dashboard changed. Every material exception remains linked to its evidence scope, actions and closure evidence.

A CAPA cannot be closed without effectiveness evidence where effectiveness is required.

## Audit record
Every automated event records detector/rule version, timestamp, affected scope, evidence references, owner, actions, approvals, closure evidence and reopen history.
