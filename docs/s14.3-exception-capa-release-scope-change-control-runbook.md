# S14.3 — Exception, CAPA and Release-Scope Change Control

## Purpose
Control deviations discovered after release authorization and ensure material changes are contained, assessed, corrected and formally authorized.

## Sequence
1. Intake the exception and preserve the originating evidence.
2. Check evidence and lineage.
3. Classify materiality.
4. Apply immediate containment for critical or potentially material issues.
5. Perform root-cause and impact assessment.
6. Define CAPA, owner, due date and acceptance criteria.
7. Assess whether the authorized release scope is affected.
8. Reopen or supersede the affected record when required.
9. Obtain independent review where required by the control matrix.
10. Test CAPA effectiveness.
11. Obtain closure authority.
12. Lock the complete audit trail.

## Exception classes
Evidence gaps, QMS corrections, custody breaks, batch traceability failures, source provenance failures, methodology/boundary/factor changes, regulatory condition changes, claim/label divergence, security/integrity events, operational changes and product-quality exceptions.

## Materiality rule
Unknown or unresolved materiality is not treated as harmless. The record remains in controlled review or HOLD until impact is established.

## Scope-change rule
A material scope change cannot be implemented by editing the historical release record. The affected authorization must be reopened or superseded and a successor record created with explicit authority and lineage.

## Evidence boundary
An exception is a control record, not an environmental observation. CAPA is corrective governance, not certification. Blockchain anchoring preserves integrity/reference continuity but does not close an exception.

## Current declaration
No exception, CAPA, scope reduction, suspension or reopening is claimed. No actual release or post-release event is asserted.
